## Alcohol Licensing

Drinks are consumed on-premises only. Beer, seltzer, and cider come in cans or bottles. Taps and wine were dropped from the concept. No state or local alcohol authority has been contacted yet.

### Beer permit

> **Research (unverified):** Tennessee's alcohol agency (TABC) says it does not license beverages under 8% alcohol by weight (10.1% by volume); the local beer board issues a beer permit for these. The application indicates on-premises, off-premises, or both, and on-premises beer sales must also meet other requirements.
>
> Inside the city, the City Council sits as the Beer Board, and the Finance Department's Business Tax Office handles applications. The applicant appears before the board. Owners and managers need police records checks (fingerprints and photographs) at the Knoxville Police Department. The permittee or on-site manager completes KAST (Knoxville Alcohol Seller Training) before issuance and annually. The board meets monthly. Applications for a meeting are due by the first of that month, and public notice runs at least ten days before. A permit not completed within ninety days of approval becomes void unless the board acts. Approvals have been conditioned on a certificate of occupancy and health department approval. An official checklist and fee schedule were not found.
>
> Outside the city limits, the Knox County Clerk issues beer permits. A source lists a $250 non-refundable fee; confirm the current amount.
>
> Sources: [MTAS Beer and Alcohol Guide 2026](https://www.mtas.tennessee.edu/system/files/mrln/mknowledge/main/MTAS%20Beer%20and%20Alcohol%20Guide%202026.pdf), [Knoxville Beer Permit](https://www.KnoxvilleTN.gov/UserFiles/Servers/Server_109478/File/Finance/Beer-Permit.pdf), [Beer Board info](https://www.knoxvilletn.gov/government/city_council/beer_board_info/beer_permit_info), [Beer Board](https://www.knoxvilletn.gov/beerboard), [Knox County Clerk beer permits](https://www.knoxcounty.org/clerk/permits_beer.php).
>
> **Verify with:** City of Knoxville Business Tax Office, 865-215-2083 (inside the city); Knox County Clerk (outside the city); TABC.

### Seltzer and cider

> **Research (unverified):** Under the state definition, "beer" is malt beverages up to 8% by weight with a cap on alcohol from flavorings. A malt-based seltzer under the limit would likely fall under a beer permit. Cider treatment is not confirmed. Ready-to-drink products that contain any distilled spirits are not beer and need a state TABC license (per a Nashville Beer Board page, which may differ from Knoxville's reading). Sources: [MTAS Beer and Alcohol Guide 2026](https://www.mtas.tennessee.edu/system/files/mrln/mknowledge/main/MTAS%20Beer%20and%20Alcohol%20Guide%202026.pdf), [Nashville beer permit definition](https://www.ctas.tennessee.edu/eli/definition-beer), [Liquor-by-the-Drink Licenses (TABC)](https://tn.gov/abc/licensing/liquor-by-the-drink-licenses.html).
>
> **Verify with:** TABC; City of Knoxville Business Tax Office, 865-215-2083; Knox County Clerk.

### Why wine was dropped

Wine was dropped after research suggested a wine license may not fit a record store.

> **Research (unverified):** On-premises wine and spirits fall under TABC liquor-by-the-drink licenses, with categories such as Restaurant, Limited Service Restaurant, Wine Only Restaurant, Club, and Hotel Motel. No plain bar or record-store category was found. A law-firm blog says an establishment under 50% food sales is a bar or limited service establishment with fees based on estimated food share, and bars and restaurants need at least 40 seats. The retail food store wine license is for off-premises sales and does not apply. Sources: [Liquor-by-the-Drink Licenses](https://tn.gov/abc/licensing/liquor-by-the-drink-licenses.html), [Retail Food Store Wine License](https://www.tn.gov/abc/licensing/retail-food-store-wine-license.html), [Dickinson Wright guide](https://www.dickinson-wright.com/news-alerts/how-to-get-a-liquor-license-in-tennessee).
>
> **Verify with:** TABC.

## Music Licensing

Music always plays in the space, either from full records on the turntable or from live performers. Live performers should prefer original music, and cover bands do not fit the vibe. The owner does not yet know how music licensing applies to the business.

> **Research (unverified):** Search results say the venue is generally responsible for licensing public performance, whether the music is recorded or live, even when performers are hired. Buying a record does not remove the requirement. No vinyl-specific treatment was found.
>
> The performing rights organizations (ASCAP, BMI, SESAC, GMR) license compositions. No single organization covers every composer, so a venue may need more than one license. Sound recordings are handled separately by SoundExchange. Fees may be based on a percentage of revenue or other factors.
>
> Original-music performers may reduce but not obviously remove licensing needs, because performers' own works may still be registered with a PRO. Not confirmed.
>
> Sources: [Texas Governor's Music Office: music in venues](https://gov.texas.gov/music/page/music_in_venues), [Texas: music licensing for your business](https://gov.texas.gov/music/page/music-licensing-for-your-business), [Boston: avoiding copyright infringement](https://boston.gov/departments/entertainment-licensing/avoiding-copyright-infringement). The Texas and Boston pages are not Tennessee sources.
>
> **Verify with:** each performing rights organization (ASCAP, BMI, SESAC, GMR) and SoundExchange, using their current terms.

### Business licenses

> **Research (unverified):** Inside the city, businesses generally need both a City of Knoxville and a Knox County business license. License type depends on expected gross receipts: a standard license for $100,000 or more, or a minimal activity license for $3,000 to $99,999.99. A source lists a $15 application fee for each. The city office is at room 450 of the City County Building, 400 Main Street. The county license comes from the Knox County Clerk. Many businesses also need a state sales tax number.
>
> The city checklist says to review the site with City of Knoxville Plans Review so the proposed activity fits the location. Certificate of occupancy steps were not found. SCORE Knoxville offers free new-business help.
>
> Sources: [Business License/Tax Office](https://knoxvilletn.gov/business/business_license_tax_office), [Knoxville business license application](https://www.KnoxvilleTN.gov/UserFiles/Servers/Server_109478/File/Finance/form_biztaxlicapp.pdf), [SCORE Tennessee requirements](https://www.score.org/tn/greater-knoxville/articles/tennessee-requirements-0/)
>
> **Verify with:** City of Knoxville Business Tax Office, 865-215-2083; Knox County Clerk, 865-215-2392; City of Knoxville Plans Review, 865-215-2999; SCORE Knoxville, 865-545-4203.
